daily salmon counts

Calculate the accumulated number of salmon over the course of each water year and aggregate over all beach seine sites. Determine a cumulative percentage for each day over the entire year and for the 3-month period January, February, and March. Plot annual accumulation curves to show the pattern of juvenile outmigration for each water year, as well as for each Jan-Mar period for each year.

# daily counts
# pivot to simplify summing Chinook counts (including 0's) for each sample unit
seine <-
  dat %>% 
  pivot_wider(
    # relies on 'sample_id' to distinguish among sample units, but retains environmental data
    id_cols = c(sample_id:time, water_temp),
    # columns for all fish spp (only CHN needed for this analysis)
    names_from = organism_code,
    # log number of each speciesxfork_length for each seine (sample_id)
    values_from = count,
    # if no matches, enter '0'
    values_fill = 0,
    # sum counts if multiple matches
    values_fn = sum
  ) %>% 
  dplyr::select(c(sample_id:water_temp, CHN))

### daily counts -----
# aggregate to daily totals bc freq >>1 seine per day
# note that this will obscure potential differences among locations

# calc cumulative percentage for each wy
daily_cum <-
  daily %>% 
  group_by(wy) %>% 
  arrange(wy, wd) %>% 
  mutate(
    annual_total = sum(daily_total),
    cum_total = cumsum(daily_total),
    cum_percent = 100 * cum_total / annual_total
  ) %>% 
  arrange(wy, month, wd) %>% 
  ungroup()

# Jan 1 is wd 93; Mar 31 is wd 182 (leap years, it's 183 but I'm going to ignore this)

I tested multiple models to explore the timing of juvenile salmon outmigration, represented by the day of the water year when three benchmarks had been reached over the course of the time series. Each model used a Gaussian distribution with water year as the predictor and the day of the water year when those benchmarks–10, 50, and 90–are reached as the response variable: day10, day50, day90. I limited the data to those gathered during January, February and March because these were sampled consistently (albeit imperfectly) in every year. Early outmigrants entering the Delta in December or earlier would be missed, but presumably the most important early numbers would be reasonably well represented.

These results present strong evidence for a year effect (i.e., model slopes were different from zero, however, the posterior distribution for the day90 model overlaps 0 slightly (95% CIs: -0.73, 0.05).

-For the 10% accumulation model (day10), there was an estimated slope of -0.69 days per year and the probability that the slope is less than 0 equals 0.999.

-For the 50% accumulation model (day50), there was an estimated slope of -0.70 days per year and the probability that the slope is less than 0 equals 1.000.

-For the 90% accumulation model (day90), there was an estimated slope of -0.33 days per year and the probability that the slope is less than 0 equals 0.955.
