WQ Hypotheses

  1. Turbidity will increase faster and higher during a first flush event compared to later storm events.
  2. Salinity will decrease more dramatically during first flush compared with other storms.
  3. Dissolved oxygen will decrease during first flush but recover afterwards.
  4. Chlorophyll will decrease more during first flush and stays low during later storm events.
  5. Do we need a water temperature hypothesis
  6. Other parameters we are interested in?

Code 
# Import continuous WQ station coordinates
sf_stations <- read_sf(
  here("data/processed/spatial/first_flush_spatial_data.gpkg"),
  layer = "cont_wq_stations"
)

# Import continuous WQ station metadata
df_metadata <- readRDS(here("data/processed/wq/cont_wq_metadata.rds"))

# Add station names to station coordinates
sf_stations_c1 <- sf_stations %>%
  left_join(df_metadata, by = join_by(Source, Station_ID)) %>%
  relocate(Station_Name, .after = Station_ID) |>
  mutate(across(where(is.POSIXct), date))

# Define stations in consideration
df_stations_analysis <- tribble(
  ~Source         , ~Station_ID     ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11447650" ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "SRH"           ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11447905" ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11447890" ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "PPT"           ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11313460" ,
  "DWR-NCRO (WQ)" , "B9520000"      ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11313434" ,
  "DWR-NCRO (WQ)" , "B9545800"      ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11312685" ,
  "DWR-NCRO (WQ)" , "B9525100"      ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11313405" ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11312676" ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11313315" ,
  "DWR-NCRO (WQ)" , "B9529500"      ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11313240" ,
  "DWR-NCRO (WQ)" , "B9537800"      ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "MSD"           ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11304810" ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "RVB"           ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11455420" ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11337190" ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "MAL"           ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "HON"           ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "RYC"           ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "GZL"           ,
  "DWR-CEMP"      , "SJR"           ,
  "USGS"          , "USGS-11303500"
)

# Filter to stations in consideration
sf_stations_c2 <- sf_stations_c1 |>
  right_join(df_stations_analysis, by = join_by(Source, Station_ID))

# Add info for CLC station
sf_clc <-
  tibble(
    Parameter = c(
      "Water Temperature",
      "Specific Conductance",
      "Turbidity (NTU)",
      "Chlorophyll Fluorescence"
    ),
    Start = ymd(c("1988-03-04", "1987-01-01", "1988-03-10", "1988-03-07"))
  ) |>
  expand_grid(
    Source = "DWR-O&M",
    Station_ID = "CLC",
    Station_Name = "Clifton Court Forebay",
    Latitude = 37.829800,
    Longitude = -121.557400,
    Interval = "Daily",
    End = ymd(today())
  ) |>
  st_as_sf(
    coords = c("Longitude", "Latitude"),
    crs = 4326,
    sf_column_name = "geom"
  )

sf_stations_c3 <-
  bind_rows(sf_stations_c2, sf_clc) |>
  mutate(Region = replace_na(Region, "Out of Bounds"))

# Import Region polygons
sf_regions <-
  read_sf(
    here("data/processed/spatial/first_flush_spatial_data.gpkg"),
    layer = "design_regions"
  ) |>
  filter(Region != "Out of Bounds")

# Create color palettes for station points (using Source) and region polygons
pal_stations <- plasma(n = length(unique(sf_stations_c3$Source)))
pal_regions <- color("muted")(length(unique(sf_regions$Region)))

sf_stations_c3 |>
  distinct(Source, Station_ID, Station_Name, Region, geom) |>
  mapview(
    zcol = "Source",
    col.regions = pal_stations,
    label = "Station_Name",
    layer.name = "Source",
    cex = 5
  ) +
  mapview(
    sf_regions,
    zcol = "Region",
    col.regions = pal_regions,
    layer.name = "Region",
    alpha.regions = 0.4,
    legend = FALSE
  )

Data Exploration

  1. Visual exploration of WQ time-series in relation to storm events at select stations

    1. Turbidity - most stations started around 2010
      1. Increases in relation to storm events.
      2. Is it typically highest after first event of the year? Maybe visualize the rate of change or difference in addition to raw values.
      3. How does turbidity increase downstream of SR at Freeport? Similar patterns? Is there a lag in response?
      4. Sacramento River vs. San Joaquin River - how do they compare? How often are storms occurring system-wide vs. in just one watershed?
    2. Salinity
    3. Dissolved Oxygen
    4. Chlorophyll

  2. Other visuals besides time-series plots - boxplots?

Metrics

  1. Turbidity
    1. Turbidity maxima after the onset of each storm
    2. Days between first day of storm and turbidity maxima (could help define time lags at downstream stations)
    3. Temporal rate of increase or raw difference (~1 week before storm vs. during storm at its peak)
    4. Spatial extent of turbidity increase (maybe use representative stations in each region to identify whether it increased in that area). Summarize this in some useful way.
    5. Gradient calculations between SR and SJR influenced stations
    6. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
    7. Some metrics to look at duration of elevated turbidity events in relation to storm events
  2. Salinity
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
  3. Dissolved Oxygen
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
  4. Chlorophyll
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
