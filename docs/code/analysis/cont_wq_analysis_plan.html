First Flush - Water Quality Analysis Plan

Author

Dave Bosworth

Published

August 19, 2026

WQ Hypotheses

  1. Turbidity will increase faster and higher during a first flush event compared to later storm events.
  2. Salinity will decrease more dramatically during first flush compared with other storms.
  3. Dissolved oxygen will decrease during first flush but recover afterwards.
  4. Chlorophyll will decrease more during first flush and stays low during later storm events.
  5. Do we need a water temperature hypothesis
  6. Other parameters we are interested in?

      1. Increases in relation to storm events.

      3. How does turbidity increase downstream of SR at Freeport? Similar patterns? Is there a lag in response?
      4. Sacramento River vs. San Joaquin River - how do they compare? How often are storms occurring system-wide vs. in just one watershed?

  2. Other visuals besides time-series plots - boxplots?

Metrics

  1. Turbidity
    1. Turbidity maxima after the onset of each storm
    2. Days between first day of storm and turbidity maxima (could help define time lags at downstream stations)
    3. Temporal rate of increase or raw difference (~1 week before storm vs. during storm at its peak)
    4. Spatial extent of turbidity increase (maybe use representative stations in each region to identify whether it increased in that area). Summarize this in some useful way.
    5. Gradient calculations between SR and SJR influenced stations
    6. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
    7. Some metrics to look at duration of elevated turbidity events in relation to storm events
  2. Salinity
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
  3. Dissolved Oxygen
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
  4. Chlorophyll
    1. Compare before (~1 week before storm) to during/after (~1 week after storm) for each storm grouped by region
